# Triage

Kelpie can look at the issues other people open. It checks every open issue against the issue format, researches each one that falls short against the repo, comments what it found and leaves it `needs-approval` for you. For an issue you opened yourself it goes one step further: it rewrites the body in the issue format, and marks the issue `ready-for-agent` when the rewrite passes the check.

```toml
[app.dogs.kelpie.triage]
pick = "maintainer"
```

- Kelpie's waiting label is `needs-approval`, and `needs-triage` now means go: it is the label a person puts on an issue to ask for its triage, and it is none of kelpie's triage labels, so it never reads as a decision. The labels swapped once: at its first start on a repo with the old `needs-triage`, kelpie renames it to `needs-approval`, so every issue that waited keeps waiting, and makes a new `needs-triage` for the ask. A repo with neither label gets neither until one is needed. An older kelpie started again on that repo reads `needs-approval` as no label of its own.
- Once a minute kelpie runs the issue check (`shep kelpie issue check`) on each open issue that is new or edited since it last looked, whatever `pick` says, `"off"` included. An issue a bot opened is skipped. The result is kept per issue and edit, so an unchanged issue is never checked or triaged twice. An issue of yours that passes is labelled `ready-for-agent`, and loses any `needs-approval`, with no triager call, unless an open pull request already closes it. If you take `ready-for-agent` off an issue that carried it, kelpie never puts it back, whatever you edit or however the check changes: label it again by hand to make it ready. Someone else's that passes gets `needs-approval`, except under `"off"`, and waits for you to label it ready: kelpie never does. A look makes at most 20 label or comment writes, and leaves the rest for the next one, so a first look at a large backlog takes a few minutes. A `ready-for-agent` issue that fails is held off the board, and `status` lists it under `skipped` with reason `check` and what the check refused, until an edit passes. One you labelled `ready-for-agent` by hand keeps that label: kelpie never swaps it for `needs-approval`, and it stays on beside any `needs-approval` its pass puts on. An issue that already carries another triage label, or that the board has worked on, is left as it is
- `pick` says whose failing issues the triager researches. `"off"`, the default, is no one's. `"maintainer"` is the ones you open (`git.maintainer`, else the login `gh` acts as, compared ignoring case), and `"all"` is everyone's. Any other word stops the runner
- A `taken` record an older kelpie saved still finishes its swap: `ready-for-agent` comes off, `needs-approval` goes on, and the issue gets its pass ahead of the board's pick. If the forge refuses the swap, the issue keeps `ready-for-agent` and it is tried at the next poll, so the board may start it meanwhile. While no App covers the repo nothing is swapped, since no pass could put the label back
- `wait_days` (default 7) is how many whole days kelpie waits on an author's word before it leaves the issue to you, and `max_questions` (1 to 10, default 10) is the most questions it may ask about one issue
- Four limits keep a burst of issues or one noisy author from spending the triager's budget: `per_day` (default 10) is the most triage calls in any 24 hours, `per_author_per_day` (default 2) the most on one author's issues in any 24 hours, `min_account_days` (default 30) the youngest an author's account may be, and `max_body_kb` (default 20) the most of an issue body the call is given. Each `0` turns that limit off
- What the triager may ask depends on who opened the issue, by the association GitHub gives them on the repo. You, and an `OWNER`, are asked as before: decisions with recommended answers, in the repo's terms. A `MEMBER`, `COLLABORATOR` or `CONTRIBUTOR` is asked what they saw and what they expected, and a decision only when their issue proposes one, at most 3 questions. A `FIRST_TIME_CONTRIBUTOR`, `FIRST_TIMER` or `NONE` author, and one whose association the listing did not say, is asked in plain language, one question at most, with a line on what happens next. Nobody but you is asked about logs, config, deployments or code, design choices go to you in the report and not to the author, and the comment posted to them names no file, test or criterion. Kelpie drops any question past the cap for the author's role, whatever the triager returned
- `authors` gives a login a role in place of its association, since the association says how someone relates to the repo and not what they can answer: `authors = { "qc-tester" = "reporter" }`. The roles are `maintainer`, `developer` and `reporter`, logins are compared ignoring case, and any other word stops the runner naming `triage.authors`
- `agent` (default `triager`) names the agent file that researches an issue. Its role must be `triager`. `add` writes `triager.md`, and you can edit it like any agent file
- Triage speaks as the App, so a `pick` other than `"off"` needs the GitHub App on the repo. The runner refuses to start without it and names `triage.pick`. A missing or wrong-role `triage.agent` or `agents.representative` is refused the same way
- Only an issue opened after you turned `pick` on is looked at, so an old backlog is left alone. Kelpie keeps that moment (`triage.since`) in the project's state, forgets it when `pick` is `off`, at a start or a change, and takes a new one when you turn it on again
- An issue is untouched when the issue check refused it, it carries none of `needs-info`, `ready-for-agent`, `ready-for-human`, `wontfix` and `possible-spam` (a `needs-approval` you add by hand does not count, and nor does a `ready-for-agent` the check refused), kelpie has no record of triaging it, a bot did not open it (the App included), and it was opened after the moment above
- `shep kelpie status` shows the untouched ones, oldest first, as `triage due`. The JSON has them under `triage.due`, and leaves the key out when none is due. A listing that fails changes nothing and costs no poll
- The first pass runs when the board is open and a slot is free, after a contribution call and before the board's pick, one issue at a time. The triager reads a clean view of `origin/main` with no shell and no plugins but kelpie's issue skill, and has 15 minutes
- Before that call the four limits are checked, in that order, and none of them calls a model. Your own issues and one `shep kelpie triage` asked for skip all four.
  - Past `per_day` the issue waits in `triage due` for the next free place, and is never dropped. The count is of every triager call that reached a model, saved the moment the call ends, so it survives a restart even on an issue that has no record yet, one issue can spend more than one place of it, and a call whose reply could not be read spends a place like any other. A cap above 200 is refused: kelpie keeps the last 200 finished triage records, a record that holds a call ahead of one that holds none, which is all a 24 hour count can see
  - Past `per_author_per_day` the issue gets `needs-approval` with no call and no comment, and the record says the limit held it. Logins are compared ignoring case
  - Under `min_account_days`, and with no history in the repo (GitHub counts the author as `NONE`, `FIRST_TIMER` or `FIRST_TIME_CONTRIBUTOR`), the same hold. The account's age is read once per author per start, and one kelpie cannot read is held by nothing
  - Past `max_body_kb` the body reaches the call cut at that size, with a line in the fence saying it was cut and how long the whole body was. The title is never cut
  - A `needs-approval` from a limit is final for that issue: the pass does not come back to it, and `shep kelpie triage <n>` is how you have it triaged anyway
  - The author's round is gated by the day's cap as the first pass is, since its calls spend the same budget. A round call with no free place is left for the next poll, and is never labelled over: the issue is already triaged. The author's cap and the account check decide which of an author's issues kelpie takes on, so they reach only an issue kelpie has not called on yet: a call that came to nothing cannot end an issue for good. An issue whose author's account was deleted since it went up is held by no author cap, since it has no login to count. A record that names no author, which is what a file saved before these limits existed leaves, gets it from the listing at the next poll, so your own round is not held by a limit it should skip
- With `[app.dogs.kelpie.triage.spam]` set, and no table means no check, an issue the limits leave alone then goes to a text classifier a gateway serves, before its research call. `gateway` names a gateway from kelpie's own `[gateways.<name>]` settings, `path` is the classifier's route on it, such as `/classify`, and `threshold` (default 0.8) is the probability at or above which the issue is read as spam. The classifier is given the issue's title, its body as the call is given it, the author's association and how many days the author's account has existed, and is asked one question: is this issue spam, an advertisement, or text unrelated to the project?
  - At or above `threshold` the issue gets `possible-spam`, made if the repo lacks it, and nothing else: no comment, no research call, no close, no lock and no hide. The label is the ask, and you decide. `shep kelpie triage <n>` is how you have it triaged anyway, and it takes `possible-spam` off, since asking for the issue is your answer to the read. A `possible-spam` you put on by hand stays, as any hand label does. `status` says the issue was read as spam and with what probability
  - Below `threshold` the pass goes on as it was
  - Your own issues and one `shep kelpie triage` asked for skip the check, as they skip the limits
  - A `gateway` kelpie's settings do not have, or one it cannot dial, keeps the runner from starting and names `triage.spam.gateway`. A gateway that cannot be reached, one that refuses the request, or one whose key kelpie's environment does not hold, is told once and the check is off for the rest of the run, with the limits still holding, so an outage opens no flood. The next start asks it again. An answer kelpie cannot read is told once and leaves the check on, so the next issue is still asked and that one goes on to its research call
  - Each check that answered is one line in the call ledger as role `triager`, kind `spam-check`, with the issue, no tokens and no cost. It is not a triager call: it spends no place of `per_day` or `per_author_per_day`, and adds nothing to the `triager` total in `shep kelpie usage`, while a research call the issue made before the check keeps its place in the day's count
- It comments once as the App, in the representative's voice, with the files, tests, related issues, draft criteria and any questions only the author can answer. A marker under the comment lets kelpie find it again. Then it puts the type (`bug` or `enhancement`) and priority on the issue if the repo has that label, a priority under the board's name `priority: P2` first and under the bare `P2` where your repo names them that way, then `needs-approval`, made if missing. A priority the issue already carries when the pass begins stands, as a `kind:` label does, and the pass's priority does not go on. So does a priority label you put on by hand while the pass ran. It also puts the issue's kind on as `kind:<type>`, one of the check's nine types, made if the repo lacks it, unless the issue already carries a `kind:` label. Kelpie never retitles. A `P0` is never set: it comes up as a question. On an issue you opened, a pass whose rewrite the check passes says it is ready for an agent and names the priority, and one that does not speaks to you, lists what is still yours to settle and recommends a priority, without calling the issue ready. On someone else's issue, the comment that hands it to you (a first pass with nothing to ask, the answers pass, and the pass that reads a rewritten body) ends with one line, `cc @<login>`, so you are notified. The ask comment, a duplicate close and a silent wait mention no one. When kelpie cannot read your login, set `git.maintainer`: the comment posts without the line and the log says so once
- A question the triager has a good answer for is not asked: it goes into the rewritten body as `Assumed: <the answer>. Say otherwise.`, so you can correct it by editing the issue. When the pass has a question only the author can answer, one with no good answer, and the author is one kelpie asks (you, on an issue you opened, or anyone at all under `pick = "all"`), the comment ends with the questions, numbered, each with the answer kelpie's research recommends, at most `max_questions` of them. Below them, in a collapsed section, is one prompt to paste into an agent session of the author's own, built from the `grill-with-docs` skill (mattpocock/skills; kelpie's own copy is at `skills/skills/engineering/grill-with-docs`). While it waits, no status label goes on: the type and priority wait until the round ends. No comment from the author within `wait_days` puts the labels on, leaves the issue `needs-approval` and posts nothing more, so the first comment stays your starting point
- Any comment from the author is their answers, whatever it says: they need no keyword, and a reply that starts with `github` or answers only some questions is read the same way. The triager reads the issue a second time with the questions and the answers, and posts again only what the answers left unsettled. For an issue you opened, a rewrite the check passes replaces the body and puts `ready-for-agent` on, and any other goes under your text. For one someone else opened, what their answers settle goes under their own text in the issue body, never in place of it, and the issue ends `needs-approval` with what is still open listed in the comment. One round of questions is the whole of it: there is no second round. A label you or someone else put on by hand, or closing the issue, ends the wait with no post and no label, a label put on while the questions comment was being worded included. A `needs-approval` the issue already carried when the questions went up does not: the wait is measured from when the questions comment went up, so an answer written right after it is heard
- The prompt names the issue, tells the session to read it with `gh issue view <issue> --comments` and the files the triage comment named, lists the open questions with the answers kelpie's research recommends, and ends by having the session rewrite the issue body with acceptance criteria and a `Touches:` list and comment the decisions it made. Kelpie then reads the issue once a minute: a body that changed since the comment went up is triaged again on its new text, and that second look ends the round as the answers round does, `ready-for-agent` for an issue you opened whose rewrite the check passes and `needs-approval` for any other, with what is still open in the comment and no further ask. Kelpie's own rewrite of a body is never read as the author's edit
- An issue you opened yourself goes further. When the pass leaves no open question, the triager has written its body in the format of kelpie's issue skill, which its call loads. Kelpie runs the issue check on the issue as that rewrite and the pass's labels would leave it. If the check passes, kelpie replaces the issue's body with the rewrite (the title stays as you wrote it), puts the type, priority and kind on, takes off the `needs-approval` an earlier pass left on, and puts `ready-for-agent` last, made if the repo lacks it, so the board can take the issue as any other. If the check refuses it, the rewrite goes under your own text, never in place of it, between two hidden `kelpie:under` lines. A later pass replaces that section rather than adding another, and leaves the text around it alone. The issue ends at `needs-approval`, and after the pass kelpie checks it again and posts one comment naming what the check refused. It is not triaged again until you edit it. An issue someone else opened never goes past `needs-approval` on its own. Kelpie only says in the comment that it will do this where it can: a rewrite naming a path on this machine is dropped before the comment is worded and the issue ends at `needs-approval` with its body as you wrote it, and a rewrite your project's `publish` hook refuses is said on the issue in a short notice. A label you or someone else put on by hand stands, whenever it went on during the pass: kelpie adds nothing and leaves the body alone. A `ready-for-agent` kelpie itself already put on is not read as one you put on, and a restart finishes the labelling where it stopped
- The research also answers whether something already covers the issue. The triager is shown the open issues and the open pull requests, each pull request with the issues its body says it closes, and answers `addressed_by`: nothing, or the one open issue or pull request that does the same thing. When kelpie can check that the number is one it showed the triager, that it is open, and that it is not the issue itself, it posts one short comment naming it as `#n` with the reason, closes the issue as a duplicate, and puts no labels on it. A number it cannot check, or a cover whose kind it cannot read as an issue or a pull request, is dropped, told in the log, and the issue is triaged as any other
- An issue kelpie closed as a duplicate is not triaged again on its own if the author reopens it: its record keeps it out of the due list. `shep kelpie triage <n>` triages it again
- A reply that is not the JSON object asked for, one that parses to no triage, is repaired once: the triager's own session is resumed with the parser's error and asked for the corrected object alone, on every harness. The repair is part of the same call, so it spends no second place of the day's cap and counts as one try below, and an answer that is still no triage is a failure like any other. A reply with no JSON object at all is not repaired.
- A call or a post that fails is told once in the log and tried at the next poll. Five failures give the issue up until a restart. A restart goes on from the step an issue got to and repeats no step it finished, so a body it already replaced is not written again, and a wait on an author keeps the time it began with. Beyond the author's round, only `shep kelpie triage` posts a second comment on an issue. An issue that already holds the App's marker comment is recorded with no call, and a marker comment that carries the questions saves the wait they opened, timed from when the comment went up
- `shep kelpie triage <issue>` triages that issue next, past the `since` and author tests. It is refused when `pick` is `off`, when the issue is not open or already carries a triage label, or while its call is in flight. An issue kelpie already triaged is triaged again: a new comment is posted and replaces the record. The pass asked again reads the rounds it ran: the triager is given kelpie's earlier triage comments on the issue and their author's replies, and a question an earlier round asked and its author answered (with a line that is only `ok` for the round, or with that question's `Qn: ...` line) is not asked again. What your author wrote to a later round answers that round alone: the questions an earlier round asked and never heard an answer to are asked again. A reply in neither form settles none. An issue kelpie's classifier read as spam is triaged too, and its `possible-spam` comes off
- `shep kelpie status` shows the call in flight and each issue kelpie triaged as `triage` lines, naming an issue it closed as a duplicate and what it duplicates, and an issue a classifier read as spam with its probability. The JSON has them under `triage.running` and `triage.issues`, a closed one with its `duplicate_of` and a spam one with its `spam`. The day's count is a `triage daily: 7 of 10 used` line, with `next place in 4h` once the day is full, and the JSON has it under `triage.daily` as `used`, `cap`, `opens` and `wait`. An issue a limit held is named with the reason
- A change to the table takes effect at the runner's next wake, with no restart. It is checked as a start checks it, so a `pick` the App does not cover is refused naming `triage.pick`, and the old settings stay
- The triager judges for itself whether an issue you opened is too big for one work item, whether or not it asks to be split. If so it answers with the drafts and kelpie files each as a sub-issue of yours, in order, blocked on the drafts before it, each `ready-for-agent`, and the comment names the sub-issues to come. Your issue keeps its rewritten body and is never made `ready-for-agent`: it stays open until the sub-issues close. Where your issue asks for an implementer, such as "run each on opus-high", each draft is pinned with an `agent:<name>!` label. A name your project does not have, a draft the issue check refuses or one that names a path on this machine files none, and the comment says why. Where you named a family and not one agent, the triager picks one and says so in an `Assumed:` line to correct. On someone else's issue the triager's split is only a plan: once the pass ends kelpie posts a new comment that mentions you, lists the sub-issues it would file, and asks. Only your reply counts. A line that is only `yes` or `ok` files the split on the next poll as above, a line that is only `no` drops the plan, and any other text leaves the ask open. Nothing from the author, a writer or anyone else counts, and they get no answer. With no reply in `wait_days` the plan drops, and the issue stays as the triage left it, with its comment and `needs-approval`. The author's body is never rewritten

Reading an issue is the triager's work and reaches the repo read-only on every harness: Claude Code, pi, Codex and goose. Its calls total on a `triager` line in `shep kelpie usage`, apart from the work items.
